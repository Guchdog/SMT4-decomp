# `0x00296F18` — cursor-axis record initializer

**Ledger:** `non_matching`. The readable C++ body differs from the pinned 72-byte ARM body. The candidate compiles to 68 bytes with no relocations.

## Contract

The ARM entry takes `r0` as a pointer to an aligned, writable 0x28-byte axis record, signed `r1` as the width/start input, and raw `r2` as the metadata/count input. It writes 32-bit words in this exact order: metadata to `+0x1C`; zero to `+0x00`, `+0x04`, `+0x08`, `+0x0C`, and `+0x20`; `r1` to `+0x10`; constant 2 to `+0x14`; zero to `+0x24`; raw `r1` to `+0x18`; then `max(r1, 0)` using a signed comparison to `+0x18`; and finally 6 to `+0x00`. Thus the helper's direct final fields are flags 6, input width, start 2, clamped threshold, raw metadata, and zero index/offset. The original leaves the record pointer in raw `r0`; the compiled candidate does the same under the tested ARMv6K toolchain.

The C++ uses volatile word stores and preserves the original store ordering, including the temporary unclamped write at `+0x18`. The supported domain is aligned ordinary writable RAM. Misaligned objects, MMIO, concurrent observation, and aliasing side effects are outside this contract.
