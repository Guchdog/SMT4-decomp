# H4 Navi divisor source: 0x0039FA68

Static ARM analysis of pinned `code.bin` SHA-256 `1f98884fe997af22e032a14c51a46c872b05b0e079a0f29e307b0e059cfaa558`; ledger gives 0x0039FA68 a 20-byte matching span, including its literal. No build/test/emulator/save access or guest write occurred. Register operations and offsets below are direct instruction observations; types, lifetime and execution domains need separate proof.

## Exact accessor ABI

ARM entry 0x0039FA68 is word-aligned, no Thumb bit. `LDR r0,[pc,#8]` at 0x0039FA68 loads the literal 0x0044CDF0 at 0x0039FA78; `LDR r0,[r0]` at 0x0039FA6C loads the current host pointer; `VLDR s0,[r0,#0x34]` at 0x0039FA70 loads its 32-bit floating-point bit pattern; `BX lr` returns at 0x0039FA74. Incoming r0 is overwritten and not dereferenced. Result is in VFP s0; r0 is also clobbered with the host pointer. No store, explicit stack use, null/range check, floating-point arithmetic or finite/nonzero guard exists here. The slot and host must be readable; invalid/null/misaligned behavior and FPSCR/fault details are not established. Returning NaN, infinity, signed zero or arbitrary bits is not precluded by this body.

## Initializer, known readers and bounded scan

At 0x00291CAC, ARM original sets allocation arguments r0=0x100, r1=0x100, r2=0, r3 loaded as 0x12344, stack word 1 and calls 0x002C4B84 at 0x00291CDC. It stores the returned r0 into slot 0x0044CDF0 at 0x00291CF0 without a null check. `VLDR s1` at 0x00291CEC loads literal 0x3F800000 at 0x00291E24, and `VSTR s1,[r0,#0x34]` at 0x00291D14 writes the 32-bit representation of **1.0f**. It is an unconditional store along the straight-line initializer path after allocation, assuming execution and a valid returned object. The same initializer writes zero float at +0x18/+0x38/+0x3C. This is initialization evidence, not proof the current host has completed initialization or remains unchanged.

An aligned ARM B/BL immediate scan across executable `[0x00100000,0x003FE624)` finds direct BLs to 0x0039FA68 at 0x0039CA38 and 0x0039CAA0, both within 0x0039C944; direct BLs to initializer 0x00291CAC at 0x001924B4 (0x001924A8), 0x001B87F4 (0x001B7E78), and 0x001D4370 (0x001D3D40). These are static sites, not proof of dynamic execution. At 0x0039CA38 the caller passes its r6, but accessor ignores it; its returned s0 is saved into s16, then the caller divides scaled floats at object +0x3C and +0x40 by s16 at 0x0039CA64 and 0x0039CA7C. The other accessor call at 0x0039CAA0 feeds the coordinate path. No zero, NaN or infinity comparison is inserted between accessor and these divides. The multiplier at 0x0044CDC8 is independently loaded before the first call and is not the divisor. FPSCR rounding, NaN propagation, exception and divide-zero behavior are not characterized; neither the initializer's 1.0 nor a single possible sample proves a general finite nonzero domain.

A bounded aligned-word scan of pinned text found 43 literal occurrences of 0x0044CDF0. I disassembled ledger functions containing those occurrences in ARM mode, looking for the immediate `#0x34`. The only **confirmed** direct host +0x34 load/store in this subset is accessor 0x0039FA70 and initializer store 0x00291D14; unrelated PC-relative instructions whose displacement happens to be 0x34 are not host fields. This is not exhaustive: a pointer may be obtained through arguments, another global, copied into registers or memory, arithmetic, a helper, an indirect/Thumb path or code beyond this scan. It does not establish a sole writer. 0x0039FA54 and 0x0039FA7C write host +0x28 and +0x2C respectively, not +0x34.

## Accepted run-004 scope

The accepted callback trace confirms host pointer 0x086EB380 and callback object host+0x40 (0x086EB3C0) at the entry, and conditionally observed 0x0039C944 returning to 0x00399DAC. Snapshot records read four bytes at host+0x30 and four at host+0x38; the callback-object prefix starts at host+0x40. None spans host+0x34, so those captures do **not** determine the divisor's sampled bits. The trace did not stop at either 0x0039FA68 call or divide; reaching the caller after 0x0039C944 alone cannot establish which inner path ran. Do not infer 1.0f for that invocation or a general valid domain from initializer and pointer observation.

## Remaining contract and next evidence

`0x0039FA68` is a small readable candidate only within a bounded contract that preserves the global slot dereference, input-r0 clobber, s0 bit representation, ABI and original fault behavior. Valid slot/host lifetime and any intervening writers remain unknown. The 0x0039C944 reconstruction additionally requires the multiplier's meaning and lifetime, sequence of global pointer changes, VFP rounding/FPSCR and behavior on zero, negative zero, NaN, infinities and boundary magnitudes, as well as accurate dependencies and memory effects. A safe next observation is a separately assigned passive breakpoint trace at accessor entry/return or divide site, with validated pointer read-back and host+0x34 bytes, path identity and s0 bits in each original/patched run; preserve per-run timestamp/order and report timeout/no-hit. This narrows one sample only. Broader static alias/writer tracing and offline bit-pattern/fault differential cases are still needed before implementation; do not use unverified guest writes or infer physical input, interaction or H4 completion.
